Microsoft

Microsoft Corporation is an American multinational technology company headquartered in Redmond, Washington, founded on April 4, 1975, by Bill Gates and Paul Allen. Leadership: The company is currently led by Satya Nadella (Chairman and CEO) and Brad Smith (Vice Chairman and President). Key Products: Microsoft is best known for the Windows operating system, the Microsoft 365 productivity suite (including Word, Excel, and PowerPoint), the Azure cloud computing platform, and the Xbox gaming division. Financial Status: It is the largest software company by revenue, with fiscal year 2025 revenues of US$281.7 billion and a market capitalization of approximately $3.6 trillion as of late 2025. History: The company rose to dominance in the PC operating system market with MS-DOS and Windows in the 1980s and 1990s, later diversifying into cloud computing and AI under Nadella’s leadership since 2014. Acquisitions: Major acquisitions include LinkedIn (2016), GitHub (2018), Skype (2011), Mojang (2014), and Activision Blizzard (2023).

Netflix

Netflix, Inc. is an American media and streaming entertainment company headquartered in Los Gatos, California. Mitsubishi

Mitsubishi refers primarily to the Mitsubishi Group (三菱グループ), a massive collective of autonomous Japanese multinational companies that share the same brand, logo, and historical roots. While they operate independently, they collectively form one of the largest industrial conglomerates in the world, accounting for an estimated 7.7% of the total revenue of all publicly traded companies in Japan. Steam

Steam is a digital distribution platform for PC games, software, and hardware owned and operated by Valve Corporation, an American video game developer headquartered in Bellevue, Washington. Founded by Gabe Newell and Mike Harrington in 1996, Valve launched Steam in September 2003 as a proprietary service to automatically update its own games, later expanding to become the largest PC game distribution platform globally with an estimated 75% market share in the early 2010s. The platform functions as a storefront, library management system, social network, and digital rights management (DRM) service, supporting Windows, macOS, Linux, iOS, and Android. Valve also manufactures consumer hardware for the platform, including the Steam Deck handheld console, Valve Index VR headset, Steam Link streaming device, and the Steam Controller. The name "Steam" was chosen to symbolize a powerful, pervasive force that delivers content seamlessly to users, playing on the company name "Valve" and industrial themes.

Funding & Scale: Brave has raised venture capital from investors including Founders Fund, Foundation Capital, and Pantera Capital, and serves over 100 million monthly active users. Structure: It is a separate entity from other companies named "Brave," such as the South Korean internet publishing firm or the Israeli mobile gaming ad platform

Whatsapp

WhatsApp is an American instant messaging and Voice over IP (VoIP) service owned by Meta Platforms (formerly Facebook). Telegram

Telegram is a cloud-based instant messaging service founded in 2013 by brothers Pavel Durov and Nikolai Durov. Pavel Durov serves as the CEO and primary owner, having left Russia in 2014 after refusing to comply with government requests to censor content on his previous platform, VKontakte. The company is headquartered in Dubai, United Arab Emirates, and operates as a private entity that has raised approximately $150 million in funding from investors including Mubadala and Abu Dhabi Catalyst Partners. Telegram distinguishes itself with features like end-to-end encryption for "secret chats," massive group support (up to 200,000 members), and cross-device synchronization. As of 2025, Telegram surpassed 1 billion monthly active users, ranking among the most downloaded apps globally. The company generates revenue through premium subscriptions and an ad platform for public channels, while maintaining a strict policy against selling user data or the company itself.
